## Event planning should not depend on scattered WhatsApp chats

Event companies in Ghana and Nigeria often manage a large amount of information for every job: client requests, vendor details, timelines, staff assignments, payments, contracts, checklists and last-minute changes.

The problem is not usually a lack of effort. The problem is that the information can become scattered across WhatsApp chats, notebooks, spreadsheets, email threads and different team members' phones.

That becomes risky when a company is handling several weddings, corporate events, funerals, naming ceremonies, engagements or community events at the same time.

Sedifex's new **Event Management** workspace is designed to bring those moving parts into one connected system.

Instead of treating an event as a collection of separate documents, Sedifex gives the event company one workspace for the full job: from the first client brief to event-day coordination and post-event evaluation.

---

For each event, a company can keep the following areas connected:

## 1. Start every event with one clear client brief

A new event can begin with the client's main requirements in the Sedifex event workspace.

The team can keep details such as venue requirements, catering, decor, entertainment, photography, transport, accommodation, theme colours and special instructions attached to the event.

For a wedding planner in Accra or Lagos, this means the client's preferences do not have to be reconstructed every time someone joins the conversation.

The planner, operations team and other authorised staff can work from the same event record.

The client can also be given a private Sedifex portal where approved parts of the brief can be reviewed and updated without exposing the company's internal planning notes.

---

## 2. Give clients tasks without giving them access to your internal checklist

One of the most useful parts of the new event workflow is the client checklist system.

The event company can decide which checklist items should be visible to the client and which should remain internal.

For example, a wedding company may want the client to complete tasks such as:

- Confirm final colour theme
- Submit names for the wedding program
- Confirm menu choice
- Send preferred entrance song
- Approve final venue arrangement details

At the same time, internal tasks such as vendor negotiation, staff assignments, cost reviews or supplier follow-up can remain private.

When the client completes a shared task, they can mark it as done, add an optional note and send it back to the event team.

Importantly, the task does not automatically become verified. A staff member can confirm it or return it to the client with a note if something is missing.

That creates a much clearer workflow than simply receiving "done" in a chat and later discovering that the requirement was incomplete.

---

## 3. Reuse your best event checklist instead of rebuilding it every time

Event companies repeat many of the same planning steps across similar events.

A traditional wedding may require a familiar sequence of preparation. A corporate conference may follow another standard workflow. A funeral, engagement or naming ceremony may have its own repeatable structure.

Sedifex allows a company to save a customised event checklist as a reusable template and apply it to future events.

The system can preserve useful planning information such as task titles, categories, default owners, priority, notes and relative due dates.

That means a task that was due 30 days before one event can remain 30 days before the next event when the template is reused.

For a growing event company, this can help turn experience into a repeatable operating process instead of keeping the company's best workflow only in the head of the founder or lead coordinator.

---

## 4. Separate the internal Run Sheet from the client-facing Program

A major event-day mistake is treating the public program and the operations schedule as the same document.

Sedifex separates them.

### The Run Sheet

The **Run Sheet** is for the internal team, staff and vendors. It can show what happens at a particular time, who owns the task, which vendor is involved, the location and operational notes.

For example:

- 10:00 AM — Decor team completes stage setup
- 11:30 AM — Sound check with DJ and MC
- 1:00 PM — Photography team arrives
- 2:30 PM — Couple arrival coordination
- 3:00 PM — Ceremony begins

This is the document the operations team can use to run event day.

### The Program

The **Program** is the client- and guest-facing order of activities.

The event company can publish the program to the client separately. If needed, the company can require client approval or allow the client to request a change.

This separation helps protect the operational plan while still giving the client a polished view of the event flow.

## 5. Use the client portal to reduce repeated follow-up

The Sedifex client portal gives the client a simpler view of the event.

Instead of exposing the entire staff workspace, the portal focuses on areas such as:

- My Event
- Program
- My Tasks
- Updates

This can reduce the number of messages such as:

"Please send the program again."

"Which task is still pending?"

"Did you receive the information I sent?"

"Has the team approved this change?"

The client has one place to check what has been shared with them, while the event company keeps its internal operations separate.

For businesses managing multiple clients at once, that can make communication more structured and easier to track.

## 6. Keep vendors, staff and event finances connected to the same job

An event cannot be delivered by the planner alone.

There may be decorators, photographers, caterers, DJs, MCs, security teams, transport providers, venues, ushers, technicians and temporary staff involved.

Sedifex allows event companies to keep vendor and staff information connected to the event instead of spreading key operational details across unrelated conversations.

Contracts, invoices, receipts, expenses and other event financial records can also remain connected to the same business system.

This gives management a clearer picture of the event as a business job, not just as a calendar booking.

---

## 7. Review the event after it is finished

Good event companies improve after every job.

Sedifex includes post-event evaluation so the team can record what went well, what created problems and what should change next time.

For example:

- Was a vendor late?
- Did the run sheet need more buffer time?
- Which client tasks were delayed?
- Did communication break down at any point?
- Which workflow should become part of the company's reusable template?

Over time, this creates an operational history that can help the business become more consistent.

## A practical example

Imagine an event company is preparing a wedding.

The team creates the event in Sedifex and records the client brief. It applies the company's wedding checklist template, assigns internal tasks and shares selected client tasks through the portal.

The client submits required information. Staff verifies each submission.

As event day approaches, the coordinator builds the internal Run Sheet for staff and vendors. A separate Program is published to the client for review. Any requested changes can be handled without replacing the team's operational schedule.

Contracts, invoices, receipts and event expenses remain connected to the job. After the event, the team records its evaluation and improves the checklist template for the next wedding.

That is the difference between simply storing event information and building an event operating system.
